Which university and college programs do your courses open?

Tick the Ontario Grade 11 and 12 courses you have or plan to take. Pick a track to see the courses that matter for it and the programs they lead to. Your ticks carry over between tracks. Programs you meet the prerequisites for light up. The rest stay grey and show which courses are missing.

Disclaimer. This app was built using AI. The program mappings are loosely based on previously available curriculum and program requirements, and they have not been verified exhaustively. Requirements change and differ between schools, so confirm with each school before choosing courses.

Programs and their prerequisites

What this covers. The Grade 12 university (U) and university/college (M) courses that Ontario universities count toward admission, the college (C) courses that college programs name as prerequisites, and the Grade 11 course each one builds on. Course codes, titles and prerequisite links match the Ministry of Education's Course Descriptions and Prerequisites listing (updated August 2026). Focus courses in the arts and emphasis courses in technology use variant codes and are not listed separately. Workplace, open and other college-level courses are left out. They count toward the diploma but are not named as program prerequisites.

Compulsory courses. Grade 11 and Grade 12 English are compulsory for the Ontario diploma, so one English course in each grade is always ticked. The university track locks the university-level course. The college track lets you swap between college and university level. The diploma also needs one Grade 11 or 12 math credit, but any math course satisfies it, so none is ticked for you.

Becoming a teacher. Ontario classroom teachers need a university degree plus a Bachelor of Education. Concurrent Education does both together from high school. Most teachers instead finish any degree first and then apply to a two-year Bachelor of Education, so almost any university program here can lead to teaching. College programs lead to early childhood educator and educational assistant roles, not to a teaching licence.

Careers. Each program lists five common careers its graduates go into. They are typical destinations, not an official ranking, and are not drawn from employment statistics. Careers marked with a further step, such as medical school, an apprenticeship or a Bachelor of Education, need that step after the program.

Professional schools. Medicine, dentistry, pharmacy, veterinary medicine, law and the MBA are entered after university study, not from high school. Their cards show the high school courses that keep the usual first degrees open, then the later steps. They are not counted in the number of programs open. Admission tests, required university courses and seat numbers change, so check each school.

What it does not cover. Prerequisites shown are the typical set across Ontario universities and Ontario public colleges. College programs elsewhere in Canada set requirements against their own province's courses. A university-level course always satisfies a college-level requirement in the same subject, and where a college asks for Grade 11 or 12 biology or chemistry, the Grade 12 U course counts too. Individual schools differ, and some add minimum grades, supplementary applications, portfolios or auditions. Every university program also needs six Grade 12 U/M credits and a competitive average. College prerequisites were checked in October 2026 against program pages at Durham, Georgian, Mohawk, Conestoga and Lambton colleges. The skilled trades and cook and baker entries are the college programs that prepare for those trades. Licensing as an electrician, plumber or refrigeration mechanic comes through an apprenticeship, which has its own entry rules. ontariocolleges.ca does not list course prerequisites itself and sends applicants to each college's page. Confirm on OUInfo or the school's own admissions page before choosing courses. French-language programs accept FRA4U in place of ENG4U.
